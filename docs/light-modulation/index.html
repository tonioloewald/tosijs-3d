Light programs

A light's whole life as one curve. Pure, deterministic, Babylon-free: the model behind b3d-lamp's flicker, pulse, beacon and fade. To see one run on a real lamp and edit it, use curve-program's demo.

One curve, two split points

A channel is a single [0,1] → [0,1] curve spanning the lamp's entire behaviour, divided by two markers:

   0 ─────────── attackEnd ──────────── sustainEnd ─────────── 1
   |   ATTACK        |       SUSTAIN         |      DECAY      |
   | plays once,     | loops while on,       | plays once,     |
   | on switch-on    | one pass per `period` | on switch-off   |

The seams cannot jump, because there is only one curve: the value at attackEnd is reached from the left by the attack and held from the right by the sustain. So the curve's value at attackEnd IS the sustain level; there is no separate level to declare.

Flicker does not compose across regions. The decay plays what the decay region draws. A fluorescent flickers to life because its attack is drawn that way; to make a lamp flicker as it dies, draw the flicker into the decay.

The contract

  • Inverted splits collapse. If sustainEnd precedes attackEnd (only reachable by hand-editing a file; the editor clamps a drag), the sustain collapses to zero width at attackEnd: the lamp plays its attack, holds at attackEnd while on, then plays the decay from sustainEnd. This is specified, so a validator may treat it as a warning; changing it is a breaking change.
  • Two discontinuities remain. Shut off mid-loop: the decay begins at sustainEnd wherever the sustain was, so keep the two ends of the sustain near each other in value. Shut off while spinning up: the decay starts at sustainEnd however far the attack had climbed.
  • Duration is one number, divided by the markers. duration is the whole curve's time and each segment takes the share it occupies (attackEnd: 0.2, duration: 1 is a 0.2 s attack), so dragging a marker retimes the lamp. attack / period / decay override individually, for a slow fade off a fast strike.
  • No randomness. Flicker is authored: give the sustain several dissimilar peaks and a period that is not a round number. That keeps it repeatable, editable and testable.

DEFAULT_PROGRAM is up fast, hold, down fast, in one second.

Channels

channel curve value means why
brightness multiplies the base intensity — 0 off, 1 as declared "off" has to be reachable; it is the value you flicker to
range multiplies the base range a range of zero is a light that lights nothing
saturation multiplies saturation — 0 is white, saturationScale sets what 1 means saturation has a meaningful zero and reaching it is the point
hue shifts the base hue, 0.5 = unchanged, scaled by hueShiftDeg hue is circular with no meaningful zero, so an absolute mapping would discard the colour you chose

A pale lamp cannot be reddened by hue alone. Rotating a hue keeps the saturation, so #cfe8ff (saturation 0.19) turns pale orange however far you turn it. To make it die as a red ember, raise saturation too, with saturationScale above 1.

The hue shift is relative. #cfe8ff sits near 207°, so hueShiftDeg: 45 takes it to cyan; it needs about 190 to reach amber. The default 30 is for leaning, not for changing a lamp's colour.

Presets

lightPresets / lightPreset(name): steady, fluorescent, flickery, brownout, candle, fireplace. They are brightness-first: colour belongs to the lamp (a candle is warm because its diffuse is warm). The two that touch colour only lean it: a brownout pulls warm as a sagging filament does, and a fire runs yellower in its flares and redder in its lulls.

API

sampleLight(program, on, sinceChange) gives the channel values sinceChange seconds after the lamp was last switched; lightPhase and programPosition (same arguments) say where in its life it is. lightProgramSchema(), canonicalProgram and validateProgram handle a program as a document.
