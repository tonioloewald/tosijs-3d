character builder

Make a person, and watch them walk. The first steps of the character creator (CHARACTER-DESIGN.md): MakeHuman's CC0 body (human-pack), reshaped by sliders for gender, age, muscle, weight, height and ancestry (human-morph), with UAL's skeleton placed from its own joint cubes (human-rig) so it plays UAL's clips at any shape.

          playing = clip ? retargetClip(clip, own?.from ?? source, ch.skeleton, { scene, armSpaceDeg: ch.recipe?.armSpaceDeg }) : null

        speedClip = (s) => { if (playing) playing.speedRatio = s }

        const garmentStore = async (mode, fn) => {

        if (!(await characterStore().get(WORKING_CHARACTER).catch(() => null))) {

        }

          }

            tailoring = null
            tailorEditing = null

Undo and redo are the arrows at the top of the panel (⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z): every change to the character as it settles, so a slider drag or a placed grip is one step (undo-history). The clip and its playback are not steps. While the rig tool has the body, they undo the rig tool's edits, and while the tailor is open, the tailor's.

Source picks the body. A first visit starts on one of four AI-made characters at random (a woman and a man, each in underwear and in a spacesuit; CC0, rigged when they load); after that the builder keeps the one you chose. MakeHuman's is the one the sliders shape and the clothes, hair and face are made for. The others are meshes from elsewhere (Blender Studio's Human Base Meshes, Quaternius' superheroes), or your own with open a mesh… (GLB or glTF). A file skinned to UAL's skeleton already, as Quaternius' characters are, is taken as it is: its own joints and weights (rigged-body). Bind your own mesh to that skeleton in Blender and it is too; it comes with Quaternius' Universal Animation Library and Universal Base Characters (CC0, pay what you want). Any other mesh has UAL's skeleton fitted to its outline and its skin bound by bone heat (auto-rig), in a second or two. They walk, wear their own materials or the skin, and take the tailor (below); the rest of the panel is MakeHuman's and is hidden for them.

Rig… opens the rig tool on that body (its panel is on the bar): drag the markers onto the joints and it is bound again on release; add a tail, springs (a ponytail, a tail) or arm space. Done (at the top of the Rig panel) brings the body back bound from that recipe, and the recipe is saved with the character; cancel keeps the one it had. The first binding is a guess read off the body's outline.

Motion plays a clip on whatever body is shown: UAL's library (the everyday clips first), retargeted. Clips from a file… adds the clips of any skinned GLB (a Mixamo or Meshy character's, say), retargeted the same way and listed after the everyday ones, for this visit.

View (the camera on the bar) holds the body square on from any side, or follows the right hand close up through the clip, to see how an item sits in it. The rig tool uses the same menu.

Hold puts an item from Tonio's textured weapons (tonio/weapons-classic/weapons.glb, CC BY 4.0: guns, blades, a plasma blade, a spear, a buckler) in the right hand, on the hand's socket (a frame normalised to the hand's anatomy, so one grip fits any body's hand). Every item is made the same way, its origin at its grip (a _grip node), +Y up the grip and +Z forward, at its real size, so one grip holds them all. Its grip says where the item's grip point goes, turned and scaled, and is set per class of item (hold grips: pistols, rifles, heavy weapons, one- and two-handed blades, bows, shields), so placing it on one sword places every one-handed blade; the grips are kept with the character. Play a clip made for it (Pistol_Idle_Loop, the shoot loops) and check it in the hand view. The numbers are a launcher's: b3dLauncher({ socket: 'right-hand', x, y, z, rx, ry, rz, modelScale }) holds it the same way on a biped.

Character keeps your work. The figure you are working on is kept in this browser as you change it and comes back when you open the page again. Save… asks for a name (the one it has, to start with) to keep it under, and asks first before replacing another saved character. Character shows the one on screen (or not saved); pick a saved one and it loads. Revert…, rename… and delete… act on the one on screen, once it is saved: revert goes back to it as last saved, and deleting keeps the figure, unsaved. Export JSON to take it elsewhere and import JSON… to bring one in. A file carries everything the panel sets, and the garments of your own the character wears travel inside it (character-file).

Open the settings (the owl): the body sliders reshape it while it walks, since every body carries its own joints. The skin weights are bone heat's, worked out from the mesh once when the page loads. The eyes (human-face) follow the camera, and the hair (human-hair) is Quaternius' CC0 hairstyles, made for another head and fitted to this one.

The tailor is always on the bar (scissors). Open it, pick a slot and a garment for it: one you cut before opens on its outline, ready to edit; (new garment) starts one; anything else (MakeHuman's clothes, an import with no outline) is simply worn. Click out an outline over the body's front view (you draw the character's left, on your right; the other side is its mirror), and the skin inside it, front and back, becomes cloth. A rectangle from the waist to the ankles is a pair of trousers. Give it a thickness, a material and a name. There is no done or cancel: what you make is kept as you go, in this browser and worn in its slot, when you switch slot or garment or close the panel, and undo and redo on the tailor's bar (⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z) step through the edits. A new garment's outline is kept with the character until it is closed, so the tailor carries on where you left it. Forget removes one of your own.

A cut is a start, not a finished garment: export it (GLB or OBJ, the garment and the body it was made on), shape it in Blender (a collar, pockets, its own UV unwrap), and import a garment to bring it back as a choice in its slot. It is bound to the body again by its shape, so it fits every body like the rest (garment-file). Or export the body alone and model a garment from nothing beside it. In Blender, tick Include → Custom Properties when exporting, so the file still says which body it was made on.

A mesh's body (a file, the Blender or Quaternius bodies) has a tailor and a wardrobe of its own: a garment cut or imported on it is worn on that body only, and MakeHuman's clothes are MakeHuman's. Its Surfaces set each of the file's materials as made (its textures, metal, glass) or in the skin. Rig a tail before cutting round the hips: cloth is not cut from a tail's skin.

New objects come in as part of the garment. Anything hard (a knee pad, a plate, a visor) gets a rigid custom property so it keeps its shape instead of bulging with the body under it (proxy-rigid).

Its surface is made in Blender too: the garment's material comes back with its base colour, normal, roughness and alpha maps, and its material set to as made with recolour at 0 shows it untouched. Recolour above 0 tints the base colour map, read as grey.

humanBody

humanBody(scene, pack) draws a pack's body as one unrigged mesh, and update(positions) reshapes it in place: for a body that only needs to be looked at.

The pack is RIGHT-HANDED (as OBJ and glTF are), so the body sits under a container that mirrors it the way glTF's __root__ does, and stands with its feet at the container's origin. Move the body by moving root. The mesh keeps every vertex the targets move (helpers and joint cubes too), but draws only the body's triangles.
